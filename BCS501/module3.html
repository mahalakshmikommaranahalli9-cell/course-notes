1. What is Agility?

Agility is the ability of a software development team to respond effectively and quickly to changes in requirements, technology, customer needs and business conditions.

Simple Definition

Agility = Ability to adapt to change quickly while continuously delivering useful software.

Why is Agility Required?

  • Customer requirements may change.
  • Business requirements may change.
  • New technologies may appear.
  • Competitors may introduce new features.
  • Users may provide new feedback.
  • Errors may be discovered during development.

Characteristics of an Agile Team

  1. Fast response to change.
  2. Frequent software delivery.
  3. Continuous customer involvement.
  4. Team collaboration.
  5. Continuous testing.
  6. Continuous improvement.

2. Agility and the Cost of Change

A software project can experience changes at any stage. The cost of implementing a change generally increases when the change is discovered later in the development process.

Traditional Development Cost ^ | | / | / | / | / | / | / |_______/________________________> Time Requirements Maintenance Agile Development Cost ^ | | _________ | / | / | / | / |______________________________> Time Continuous feedback and adaptation

Why Does Cost Increase?

If a requirement changes after design, coding, testing and deployment, several completed activities may have to be modified.

  • Requirements may need modification.
  • Design may need modification.
  • Source code may need modification.
  • Testing must be repeated.
  • Documentation may need modification.
  • Deployment may be affected.
Exam Point: Agile methods attempt to reduce the impact of change by using short iterations, frequent customer feedback and continuous testing.

3. What is an Agile Process?

An Agile process is an iterative and incremental software development process in which software is developed in small releases and continuously improved using customer feedback.

Basic Agile Process

Customer Requirements ↓ Product Backlog ↓ Select Features ↓ Iteration ↓ Design → Code → Test ↓ Working Software ↓ Customer Feedback ↓ Improve / Change ↓ Next Iteration

Main Characteristics

  1. Iterative development: Development occurs through repeated cycles.
  2. Incremental delivery: New functionality is added incrementally.
  3. Customer collaboration: Customers provide regular feedback.
  4. Continuous testing: Testing is performed throughout development.
  5. Adaptability: Requirements can be changed when necessary.

4. Principles of Agile Software Development

Agile development is guided by principles that encourage customer collaboration, working software, communication, adaptability and continuous improvement.

Important Agile Principles

  1. Customer satisfaction: Deliver useful software early and continuously.
  2. Welcome changing requirements: Changes can be accepted even during development.
  3. Frequent delivery: Deliver working software frequently.
  4. Customer and developer collaboration: Developers and customers should work together.
  5. Motivated individuals: Give teams the environment and support they need.
  6. Face-to-face communication: Direct communication improves understanding.
  7. Working software: Working software is an important measure of progress.
  8. Sustainable development: Development should continue at a sustainable pace.
  9. Technical excellence: Good design and technical practices improve agility.
  10. Simplicity: Avoid unnecessary work and complexity.
  11. Self-organizing teams: Teams organize their work effectively.
  12. Continuous improvement: Teams regularly examine their work and improve it.

5. Extreme Programming (XP)

Extreme Programming (XP) is an Agile software development method that emphasizes frequent releases, customer involvement, continuous feedback, testing and simple design.

Goals of XP

  • Deliver software quickly.
  • Respond to changing requirements.
  • Improve software quality.
  • Maintain continuous customer involvement.
  • Reduce development risks.

XP Process

USER STORIES ↓ RELEASE PLANNING ↓ ┌───────────────────┐ │ ITERATION PLANNING│ └─────────┬─────────┘ ↓ SIMPLE DESIGN ↓ CODING ↓ UNIT TESTING ↓ CUSTOMER ACCEPTANCE TESTING ↓ SMALL RELEASE ↓ FEEDBACK ↓ NEXT ITERATION

Important XP Practices

Practice Meaning
Pair Programming Two developers work together on the same code.
Test-Driven Development Tests are created and used continuously during development.
Continuous Integration Code changes are integrated frequently.
Simple Design Keep the design as simple as possible.
Refactoring Improve internal code structure without changing its external behavior.
Small Releases Deliver useful software in small increments.
Customer Involvement Customer feedback is obtained throughout development.

Advantages of XP

  • Handles changing requirements.
  • Improves software quality.
  • Provides frequent feedback.
  • Detects defects early.
  • Encourages communication.

6. Scrum Process

Scrum is an Agile framework in which software development is performed through short, time-boxed iterations called Sprints.

Scrum Flow

PRODUCT BACKLOG ↓ SPRINT PLANNING ↓ SPRINT BACKLOG ↓ ┌─────────────────┐ │ SPRINT │ │ │ │ Plan → Develop │ │ Test → Review │ └────────┬────────┘ ↓ WORKING PRODUCT ↓ SPRINT REVIEW ↓ SPRINT RETROSPECTIVE ↓ PRODUCT BACKLOG ↓ NEXT SPRINT

Scrum Roles

Role Responsibility
Product Owner Represents product requirements and manages product priorities.
Scrum Master Helps the team follow Scrum practices and removes obstacles.
Development Team Builds and tests the product increment.

Important Scrum Events

  • Sprint Planning
  • Daily Scrum
  • Sprint Review
  • Sprint Retrospective

7. Other Agile Process Models

Apart from XP and Scrum, several Agile approaches can be used depending on project requirements and team structure.

1. Adaptive Software Development (ASD)

ASD focuses on adapting to changing requirements. Its basic activities can be viewed as:

SPECULATE ↓ COLLABORATE ↓ LEARN ↓ NEXT ITERATION

2. Dynamic Systems Development Method (DSDM)

DSDM is an Agile approach that emphasizes business needs, active user involvement and frequent delivery.

3. Scrum

Scrum uses Sprints, product backlog, sprint backlog, reviews and retrospectives.

4. Crystal

Crystal is a family of Agile methods that gives importance to communication, teamwork and frequent delivery.

5. Feature-Driven Development (FDD)

FDD organizes development around designing and building features.

6. Agile Modeling

Agile modeling uses lightweight modeling techniques to support Agile development without unnecessary documentation.

8. Tool Set for Agile Process

Agile teams use different tools to support planning, communication, coding, testing, integration and tracking.

Tool Category Purpose
Task Management Track user stories, tasks and project progress.
Version Control Manage source code changes and collaboration.
Continuous Integration Automatically build and test code changes.
Testing Tools Automate software testing and defect detection.
Communication Tools Support team communication and collaboration.
Issue Tracking Record, assign and monitor defects and tasks.
Simple memory trick: Plan → Code → Test → Integrate → Track → Communicate

9. Software Engineering Knowledge

Software engineering knowledge refers to the principles, methods, techniques and practices used to develop and maintain high-quality software.

Important Areas

  • Requirements engineering
  • Software design
  • Software construction
  • Software testing
  • Software maintenance
  • Project management
  • Configuration management
  • Quality management
Exam idea: Software engineering knowledge helps developers choose suitable processes and technical practices for solving software problems systematically.

10. Core Principles

Core principles provide general guidance for developing software effectively.

Important Core Principles

  1. Be agile: Accept and manage changes effectively.
  2. Focus on quality: Build quality into every development activity.
  3. Keep it simple: Avoid unnecessary complexity.
  4. Communicate: Maintain effective communication among stakeholders.
  5. Plan: Establish objectives, tasks and priorities.
  6. Build for reuse: Reusable components can reduce development effort.
  7. Review: Regularly evaluate work products.
  8. Test continuously: Testing should not be postponed until the end.

11. Principles Guiding Each Framework Activity

Software engineering framework activities provide a structured way of developing software.

1. Communication

  • Listen carefully to stakeholders.
  • Identify the actual problem.
  • Encourage collaboration.
  • Clarify requirements.

2. Planning

  • Understand project objectives.
  • Identify tasks.
  • Estimate resources.
  • Identify risks.
  • Establish schedules.

3. Modeling

  • Keep models understandable.
  • Model the problem before implementation.
  • Avoid unnecessary modeling.
  • Maintain consistency.

4. Construction

  • Write understandable code.
  • Follow coding standards.
  • Perform continuous testing.
  • Review code regularly.

5. Deployment

  • Deliver useful software.
  • Collect customer feedback.
  • Correct defects.
  • Improve future releases.
COMMUNICATION ↓ PLANNING ↓ MODELING ↓ CONSTRUCTION ↓ DEPLOYMENT ↓ FEEDBACK ↓ NEXT ITERATION

12. Agile vs Traditional Development

Agile Traditional
Iterative and incremental Usually sequential and plan-driven
Welcomes changing requirements Changes may require formal processes
Frequent releases Often larger releases
Continuous customer feedback Customer involvement may be concentrated in specific phases
Continuous testing Testing may be concentrated later in the lifecycle
Flexible planning More emphasis on upfront planning

14. ✍️ 2-Mark Questions & Answers

1. What is Agility?
Agility is the ability of a software team to respond quickly and effectively to changing requirements, technology and customer needs.
2. What is Agile Process?
Agile process is an iterative and incremental software development process that emphasizes frequent delivery, customer collaboration and adaptation to change.
3. What is XP?
Extreme Programming is an Agile software development method that emphasizes customer involvement, continuous testing, simple design and frequent releases.
4. What is Scrum?
Scrum is an Agile framework that organizes development into short iterations called Sprints.
5. What is pair programming?
Pair programming is an XP practice in which two developers work together on the same programming task.
6. What is refactoring?
Refactoring is the process of improving the internal structure of code without changing its external behavior.
7. What is a Sprint?
A Sprint is a short, fixed development period in Scrum during which a usable product increment is created.
8. What is continuous integration?
Continuous integration is the frequent integration of code changes into a shared codebase followed by automated or regular verification.
