📚 Module 4 — Introduction to Project Management

Software Project Management deals with planning, organizing, monitoring and controlling software projects so that the project objectives can be achieved within agreed constraints.

Simple Definition: Software Project Management = Plan + Organize + Monitor + Control + Deliver

1. Introduction to Project Management

A project is a temporary activity undertaken to create a specific product, service or result.

A software project involves activities such as requirements analysis, design, coding, testing, deployment and maintenance.

Project Management

Project management is the application of knowledge, skills, tools and techniques to project activities to achieve project requirements and objectives.

Typical Project Constraints

PROJECT │ ┌───────────┼───────────┐ ↓ ↓ ↓ TIME COST SCOPE │ │ │ └───────────┼───────────┘ ↓ QUALITY

A project manager must balance factors such as scope, time, cost, quality, resources and risk.

2. Importance of Project Management

Software projects involve people, technology, money, schedules and changing requirements. Project management provides a systematic way to coordinate these elements.

Importance

  • Helps define clear project objectives.
  • Provides proper planning.
  • Controls project cost.
  • Controls project schedule.
  • Manages project risks.
  • Coordinates team members.
  • Monitors project progress.
  • Helps maintain quality.
  • Improves communication with stakeholders.
  • Supports successful project delivery.

3. Contract Management

A contract is a formal agreement between parties that defines obligations, responsibilities, deliverables, payments and other conditions.

Contract management is the process of managing the contract from its creation through completion.

Stages of Contract Management

Identify Requirement ↓ Prepare Contract ↓ Select Supplier / Contractor ↓ Negotiate Terms ↓ Contract Agreement ↓ Contract Execution ↓ Monitor Performance ↓ Manage Changes ↓ Contract Completion ↓ Review / Closure

Important Activities

  • Define requirements.
  • Prepare contract specifications.
  • Negotiate price and terms.
  • Define responsibilities.
  • Monitor supplier performance.
  • Manage changes and disputes.
  • Verify deliverables.
  • Complete contract closure.

4. Activities Covered by Software Project Management

Software Project Management covers several activities required to plan and control the project.

SOFTWARE PROJECT MANAGEMENT │ ┌────────────┼─────────────┐ ↓ ↓ ↓ Planning Monitoring Control │ │ │ ↓ ↓ ↓ Estimation Progress Corrective Scheduling Tracking Actions │ ├── Resources ├── Cost ├── Time ├── Quality └── Risk

Main Activities

  1. Project planning: Define what must be done and how.
  2. Estimation: Estimate effort, cost and resources.
  3. Scheduling: Assign activities and establish time frames.
  4. Resource management: Allocate people, hardware and software resources.
  5. Risk management: Identify and manage possible risks.
  6. Quality management: Ensure required quality standards are followed.
  7. Monitoring: Compare actual progress with planned progress.
  8. Control: Take corrective actions when deviations occur.

5. Plans, Methods and Methodologies

Project Plan

A project plan describes how the project will be executed, monitored and controlled.

Typical Contents

  • Project objectives
  • Scope
  • Schedule
  • Resources
  • Budget
  • Quality requirements
  • Risk management
  • Communication plan
  • Configuration management

Methods

Methods are organized approaches used to perform project activities or solve specific project problems.

Methodology

A methodology provides a structured set of processes, practices, guidelines and activities for managing and developing a project.

7. Stakeholders

A stakeholder is a person, group or organization that can affect, be affected by, or have an interest in the project.

Examples

  • Customer
  • Project manager
  • Software developers
  • Testers
  • End users
  • Management
  • Suppliers
  • Government or regulatory bodies
STAKEHOLDERS │ ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐ ↓ ↓ ↓ Customer Project Users Manager ↓ ↓ ↓ Requirements Team Feedback

8. Setting Project Objectives

Project objectives describe what the project is expected to achieve.

Characteristics of Good Objectives

  • Specific — clearly defined.
  • Measurable — progress can be measured.
  • Achievable — realistically possible.
  • Relevant — related to project goals.
  • Time-bound — has a defined time frame.

Procedure for Setting Objectives

Identify Business Need ↓ Understand Stakeholder Needs ↓ Define Project Scope ↓ Define Specific Objectives ↓ Identify Constraints ↓ Define Measures of Success ↓ Review and Approve ↓ Monitor Objectives
Example: Develop and deploy a college attendance system that allows faculty to record attendance and students to view their attendance within the planned project period.

9. Business Case

A business case provides justification for undertaking a project. It explains why the project is needed and whether the expected benefits justify the required investment.

Contents of a Business Case

  • Business problem or opportunity
  • Project objectives
  • Expected benefits
  • Estimated cost
  • Resources required
  • Major risks
  • Possible alternatives
  • Financial evaluation
  • Recommendation / decision

10. Project Success and Failure

Project Success

A software project can be considered successful when it satisfies its agreed objectives and stakeholder expectations within applicable constraints.

Factors Supporting Success

  • Clear objectives
  • Good requirements
  • Effective planning
  • Skilled team
  • Good communication
  • Effective risk management
  • Customer involvement
  • Quality assurance

Common Causes of Failure

  • Unclear objectives
  • Poor requirements
  • Unrealistic schedules
  • Poor estimation
  • Insufficient resources
  • Weak communication
  • Unmanaged risks
  • Uncontrolled scope changes

11. Management and Management Control

Management involves planning, organizing, directing and controlling project resources to achieve project objectives.

Management Control

Management control involves comparing actual project performance with planned performance and taking corrective action when necessary.

PROJECT PLAN ↓ ACTUAL WORK ↓ MEASURE PROGRESS ↓ COMPARE WITH PLAN ↓ ┌────┴────┐ ↓ ↓ On Track Deviation ↓ ↓ Continue Corrective Action ↓ Update Plan

12. Project Management Life Cycle

The project management life cycle describes the major stages through which a project passes from beginning to completion.

INITIATION ↓ PLANNING ↓ EXECUTION ↓ MONITORING & CONTROL ↓ CLOSURE

1. Initiation

  • Identify the project.
  • Understand the business need.
  • Identify stakeholders.
  • Define initial objectives.
  • Assess feasibility.

2. Planning

  • Define scope.
  • Prepare schedule.
  • Estimate cost.
  • Allocate resources.
  • Plan quality.
  • Identify risks.

3. Execution

  • Perform planned project activities.
  • Develop software.
  • Coordinate team members.
  • Manage resources.

4. Monitoring and Control

  • Measure project progress.
  • Compare actual and planned performance.
  • Monitor cost and schedule.
  • Manage risks.
  • Take corrective action.

5. Closure

  • Complete deliverables.
  • Obtain acceptance.
  • Close contracts.
  • Document lessons learned.
  • Release project resources.

14. Project Evaluation

Project evaluation is the process of assessing whether a proposed or completed project is worthwhile and whether it meets specified objectives.

Evaluation of Individual Projects

Before selecting a project, management may examine technical, financial, organizational and strategic factors.

Important Evaluation Factors

  • Expected benefits
  • Development cost
  • Operating cost
  • Technical feasibility
  • Available resources
  • Project risks
  • Strategic importance
  • Expected return
  • Time required

15. Cost-Benefit Evaluation Techniques

Cost-benefit evaluation compares the expected costs of a project with its expected benefits.

Basic Idea: If expected benefits justify the costs and risks, the project may be considered economically attractive.

1. Payback Period

Payback period is the time required for the accumulated benefits or cash inflows to recover the initial investment.

Simple formula:

Payback Period = Initial Investment ÷ Annual Cash Inflow

2. Return on Investment (ROI)

ROI measures the return obtained relative to the investment.

Formula:

ROI = (Net Benefit ÷ Investment) × 100

3. Net Present Value (NPV)

NPV considers the time value of money by converting future cash flows into their present value.

Basic idea:

NPV = Present Value of Benefits − Present Value of Costs

4. Internal Rate of Return (IRR)

IRR is the discount rate at which the NPV of a project becomes zero.

5. Cost-Benefit Ratio

The cost-benefit ratio compares the value of expected benefits with the associated costs.

PROJECT │ ┌───────┴────────┐ ↓ ↓ COSTS BENEFITS │ │ └───────┬────────┘ ↓ ECONOMIC EVALUATION ↓ PROJECT DECISION

16. Risk Evaluation

A risk is an uncertain event or condition that may negatively affect project objectives such as cost, schedule, scope or quality.

Risk Evaluation

Risk evaluation involves assessing identified risks based on their likelihood and potential impact.

Risk Evaluation Process

Identify Risk ↓ Estimate Probability ↓ Estimate Impact ↓ Determine Risk Exposure ↓ Prioritize Risks ↓ Plan Response ↓ Monitor Risk

Risk Exposure

A simple way to represent risk exposure is:

Risk Exposure = Probability × Impact

Example

Suppose a project has a 20% probability of a particular risk occurring and the estimated loss is ₹50,000.

Risk Exposure = 0.20 × ₹50,000

Risk Exposure = ₹10,000

Risk Responses

Response Meaning
Avoid Change the plan to eliminate the risk.
Reduce / Mitigate Reduce probability or impact.
Transfer Shift some responsibility or financial impact to another party.
Accept Acknowledge the risk and monitor or manage it.

17. Software Projects vs Other Projects

Software Projects Many Physical/Traditional Projects
Product is mainly intangible. Product may be physically observable.
Requirements can change frequently. Changes may be more expensive after physical work.
Progress can be difficult to judge visually. Physical progress may be easier to observe.
Technology changes rapidly. Technology may be comparatively stable depending on the project.
Software can be copied and modified easily. Physical products require physical materials.
Maintenance and evolution are major concerns. Maintenance depends on the type of physical project.
